LumoSnap desktop · design log · 29 Oct 2026

Delivery tracking for photographs

The mock shows every signed-in and signed-out screen of the app in one file. Each album is a parcel on its way from your disk to your client, and the app always shows where it is. The look comes from the 5 reference images: grey canvas, white cards, one black pill, and blue from the sleep-bar reference. Open lumosnap-mock.html to use it.

Brief

Who uses it, for which job, in which moment.

  • Who sets the look: Existing, at Reimagine level. The requester asked for a cleaner, less generated look. The 5 reference images give the look, so the look is also Given.
  • Scope: product. All screens in fe-design-flow.md, as one connected mock.
  • For: independent photographers and small studios in India who shoot weddings, portraits and commercial work.
  • Job: move a shoot from a folder on disk to a private client link, then see what the client picked.
  • Moment: after the edit, often late, with a large upload in the background and a client who waits on WhatsApp.
  • Character: delivery tracking for photographs. Each album is a parcel. It is on disk, in transit, or delivered, and the app always says which.
  • Feel: calm and sure. In the first second the photos are the only colour. The app says "your work is handled", not "look at this app".
  • Richness: medium. The library and the gallery are full of photos, so the chrome stays plain. The progress surfaces get the richest detail because the photographer checks them most.
  • Identity: kept from the product: the violet aperture logo, Outfit (already bundled as @fontsource/outfit). New: blue only for progress, data, links and selection.
  • Core copy: "New album", "Share with client", "Client link", "Picked by", "Folder changed", "Folder missing", "Uploading 318 of 480", "Review folder changes", "Sync 25 changes".
  • First value: on the empty library, one action ("Create your first album") makes a real album with a real folder. A first-run sample album is not in the spec, so the mock does not add one.
  • Signature moment: the upload progress: a pill in the Albums header that opens a panel with the percent, a segmented bar and the stage counts. Failed files show as red notches on the bar.
  • Delight piece: the print stack, in the sidebar above the plan. Every delivered photo becomes a 6×4 print on a pile. The top print is the newest album cover, and the line says how tall the pile is (0.3 mm a print) next to a light stand. It needs no new back end: it uses album covers and photo counts the app already has. Two earlier tries on 30 Oct were removed. A 2 × 2 grid of agreed frames was the genre's favourites grid. A delivery record needed link and open tracking, which the product does not have, and it was a feature, not a feeling.
  • Ending: upload ends with "All 480 uploaded" in the pill, then the pill goes away. Sync ends with the thumb at "Cloud" and "In sync". Share ends with "Copied" on the button.
  • Screens: 960 px to 2560 px, desktop only. Source: Electron window 1400 × 900 in src/main/index.ts, no phone target.
  • Map: Albums is home. An album opens its gallery; the gallery leads to the lightbox and to client notes. Bookings and Settings are side places. The account and plan usage sit at the foot of the sidebar.
  • Flow shape: setup is 3 steps (one decision each). Create album, delete and sync are dialogs on top of Albums, because each is one short decision about one album.
  • Constraints: keep every function in the spec; no new back-end data.

Direction

Delivery tracking, not the contact sheet, because the ledger already holds a contact-sheet run for this product.

  • Default to beat: white #fff page, slate-200 borders, indigo-500 accent, Inter 14 px, 8 px radius cards with a small shadow, top bar plus card grid, title-case labels, gradient on the active nav item.
  • Directions sampled:
    • Delivery tracking (chosen): progress and place are the main facts. Typicality about 15%.
    • Darkroom: dark grey surround like an editing app. Typicality about 45%. Beat: the references are light, and clients judge photos on light pages.
    • Gallery wall labels: much white space, small serif captions. Typicality about 25%. Beat: Bookings and Settings need density.
  • Rolled candidate: lab order envelope: kraft paper, stamped order numbers. Beat: on an Operate surface it is a costume.
  • Chosen: delivery tracking inside the given look. Mutation: remove the top bar. The account and plan usage move to the sidebar foot, so the header holds only the page and its actions.
  • Motif: the route bar: a track with two ends and a mark that moves (upload bar, sync bar, plan usage, free disk space).
  • Style tile: the setup screen and the Albums capture served as the tile. Both show the grey canvas, white cards, the black pill and one blue bar.
  • Default check: background is grey oklch(94.6%), not white. Type is Outfit 300/400/500, not Inter. Navigation is a left sidebar, not a top bar. Accent is a calm blue (the owner rejected violet), used only for progress, data, links and selection. Radius is 22 px for cards and 14 px for insets, not 8 px. The skeleton changed, so the direction landed.

Voice

  • This, not that: "photos", not "images"; "client", not "user"; "Folder missing", not "ORPHANED"; plain facts, not cheer (one exclamation mark in the old setup is gone).
  • Terms: album, photos, client, picks, client link, booking link, watch folder, sync, upload.
  • Address: "you" for the photographer; buttons are verb plus object; sentence case everywhere.

Session 2026-10-31 (6): every save says it is saving

Existing work, Minimal level, flow scope: the pending, failure and empty states of the async actions added on this branch (People, tags, sets, gallery reload). The look is kept. The photographer reported that saving a new name in the pick view gave no sign of work. Screens: 960 px to 2560 px, desktop only.

  • For: the photographer, often on venue Wi-Fi or on battery, naming people and sorting photos in short bursts.
  • Job: know that a click was taken, know when it failed, and get back to where they were.
  • Rule: quiet when fast, clear when slow. A save that ends in less than 300 ms shows nothing new. A slower one shows the app's own spinner on the control that was pressed.

Session 2026-10-31 (5): People, pick from a photo

Existing work, Revamp level, flow scope: the People card, a new "pick people from a photo" view, a new "Is this Anjali?" check, and the person menu. The look is kept. The flow changes: the app no longer shows a wall of faces with no names. The photographer names the 4 to 8 people who matter on one group photo, and the app finds their other faces. Screens: 960 px to 2560 px, desktop only.

  • For: the photographer after the edit, with a client who asks "send me all the photos of my father".
  • Job: name a few people fast, then filter by them. Fix the doubtful matches in batches, not one by one.
  • Signature moment: naming on the photo. Type a name, press Enter, and the next face opens. Seven faces take seven names and seven Enters.

  • Mutation: no "Not named yet" faces at all. The app shows only people the photographer named.
  • Ending: naming ends with "Everyone here is named" and focus on Done. The check ends with the toast "Anjali's faces are all checked" and the count goes away from the avatar.
